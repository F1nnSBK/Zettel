# On the Limits of Post-Hoc Matryoshkization: Riemannian Flag Optimality and the Spherical Subspace Distortion Bound

**Author:** Finn Hertsch

*Duale Hochschule Baden-Württemberg (DHBW) Ravensburg*

---

### Abstract

Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) enforces nested, capacity-adaptive sub-vector representations during end-to-end training of deep neural networks. Adapting existing frozen foundation models to hierarchical retrieval typically demands prohibitive retraining budgets. This paper investigates whether an unorganized latent space can be restructured *post-hoc* into a nested sequence of subspaces without modifying backbone parameters. We formalize post-hoc representation ordering as an optimization problem over the Flag manifold $\mathrm{Fl}(d_1, \dots, d_K; \mathbb{R}^D)$. We prove the **Spherical Flag Distortion Bound Theorem**, establishing a rigorous analytical upper bound for cosine distortion under subspace truncation:  
$$
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{ij}^{(d_k)}] \le \frac{3}{1-c} \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k,
$$
governed purely by the residual spectral tail energy $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k$. We resolve the **Extrinsic Curvature Gap**: while submanifolds with vanishing second fundamental form can be linearly embedded without geometric distortion, those with non‑zero extrinsic curvature force the affine span to exceed the intrinsic dimension, creating an irreducible residual error that mathematically separates linear post-hoc rotations from native non-linear MRL. Finally, while linear trace objectives trivially reduce to Principal Component Analysis (PCA) via the Ky Fan theorem, we demonstrate that minimizing true spherical cosine distortion requires Riemannian optimization, for which we present a Flag gradient descent scheme using Cayley retractions.

### 1. Introduction

High-dimensional dense embeddings produced by modern foundation models across computer vision and natural language processing are rotationally arbitrary. Downstream approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) databases and high-throughput vector systems require compact, low-dimensional prefixes to maximize memory bandwidth and search efficiency. However, truncating arbitrary dimensions of an unregularized embedding severely degrades retrieval accuracy.

Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) resolves this by enforcing multi-scale prefix losses during model training. When foundation backbones cannot be retrained due to computational constraints, post-hoc methods are required. Classical dimensionality reduction, such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) or Optimized Product Quantization (OPQ), optimizes Euclidean variance or quantization error in $\mathbb{R}^D$. These methods do not directly optimize angular preservation on the unit hypersphere $S^{D-1}$, where normalized contrastive embeddings operate.

This work addresses three foundational questions:

1. What is the exact mathematical bound on angular distortion when projecting a spherical representation space into nested subspaces via orthogonal transformations?
2. What geometric properties prevent linear post-hoc rotations from matching the empirical performance ceiling of native, non-linear MRL?
3. When does post-hoc alignment reduce to a closed-form spectral decomposition (PCA), and when does it necessitate iterative Riemannian optimization over Flag manifolds?

### 2. Geometric Preliminaries & Problem Formulation

Let $\mathcal{Z} = \{z_1, \dots, z_N\} \subset S^{D-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^D$ denote unit-normalized embeddings generated by a frozen backbone $f_\theta$. The exact cosine similarity between pairs is:

$$
s_{ij} = \langle z_i, z_j \rangle = z_i^\top z_j
$$

**Definition 2.1 (The Flag Manifold)**

Let $d_1 < d_2 < \dots < d_K = D$ be an ordered sequence of target capacities. A sequence of nested linear subspaces forms a flag $\mathcal{F}$:

$$
\{0\} \subset V_{d_1} \subset V_{d_2} \subset \dots \subset V_{d_K} = \mathbb{R}^D, \quad \dim(V_{d_k}) = d_k
$$

The space of all such flags defines the Flag Manifold:

$$
\mathrm{Fl}(d_1, \dots, d_K; \mathbb{R}^D) \cong \frac{\mathrm{O}(D)}{\mathrm{O}(d_1) \times \mathrm{O}(d_2 - d_1) \times \dots \times \mathrm{O}(D - d_{K-1})}
$$

A flag can be parameterized by an orthogonal rotation matrix $R \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ satisfying $R^\top R = I_D$.

**Definition 2.2 (Truncated Spherical Projection)**

Let $\Pi_{d_k}: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ denote the coordinate truncation operator $\Pi_{d_k}(v) = (v_1, \dots, v_{d_k})^\top$. For an orthogonal matrix $R \in \mathrm{O}(D)$, the $d_k$-dimensional normalized subspace representation $\tilde{z}^{(d_k)} \in S^{d_k-1}$ is:

$$
\tilde{z}^{(d_k)} = \frac{\Pi_{d_k}(R z)}{\lVert \Pi_{d_k}(R z) \rVert_2}
$$

**Definition 2.3 (Spectral Tail Energy)**

The residual spectral energy discarded by truncation at dimension $d_k$ is:

$$
\mathcal{E}_k(R; z) = 1 - \lVert \Pi_{d_k}(R z) \rVert_2^2 = \sum_{m=d_k+1}^D (R z)_m^2
$$

We denote the expected spectral tail energy over the data distribution $\mu$ as $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mu}[\mathcal{E}_k(R; z)]$.

```text
[ Ambient Space S^(D-1) ] ── True Metric: s_ij = <z_i, z_j>
             │
             ▼  Orthogonal Rotation R ∈ O(D)
[ Flag-Aligned Space R z  ]

             ▼  Subspace Truncation & Normalization

```

#### Theorem 1 (Spherical Flag Distortion Bound)

Let $z_i, z_j \sim \mu$ be independent unit-normalized embeddings on $S^{D-1}$ with true cosine similarity $s_{ij} = \langle z_i, z_j \rangle$. Let $\tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)} = \langle \tilde{z}_i^{(d_k)}, \tilde{z}_j^{(d_k)} \rangle$ be the cosine similarity evaluated in the truncated subspace $S^{d_k-1}$. Assume the spectral tail energy is strictly bounded such that $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{z \sim \mu} \mathcal{E}_k(R; z) \le c < 1$.

The expected absolute metric distortion $\Delta_{ij}^{(d_k)} = |s_{ij} - \tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)}|$ satisfies the strict linear bound:

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[ \Delta_{ij}^{(d_k)} \right] \le \frac{3}{1-c} \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R)
$$

**Proof of Theorem 1:**

Because $R \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ is an isometry, we decompose the inner product into active and tail components:

$$
s_{ij} = \langle \Pi_{d_k}(R z_i), \Pi_{d_k}(R z_j) \rangle + \langle t_i, t_j \rangle
$$

where $t = \Pi_{D-d_k}(R z)$. Let $u = \Pi_{d_k}(R z)$. The truncated normalized inner product is:

$$
\tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)} = \frac{\langle u_i, u_j \rangle}{\lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2} = \frac{s_{ij} - \langle t_i, t_j \rangle}{\lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2}
$$

Subtracting $s_{ij}$ yields:

$$
\tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)} - s_{ij} = \frac{s_{ij} (1 - \lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2) - \langle t_i, t_j \rangle}{\lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2}
$$

Taking the absolute value, bounding $|s_{ij}| \le 1$, and applying the triangle inequality:

$$
|\tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)} - s_{ij}| \le \frac{1 - \lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2 + |\langle t_i, t_j \rangle|}{\lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2}
$$

Note that $\lVert u \rVert_2 = \sqrt{1 - \mathcal{E}}$. For $x, y \in [0, 1]$, the algebraic inequality $\sqrt{(1-x)(1-y)} \ge 1 - x - y$ holds strictly (proof: if $x+y \le 1$, squaring both sides reduces to $x+y \ge (x+y)^2$, which is true for $x+y \in [0,1]$; if $x+y > 1$, the right-hand side is negative and the inequality is trivial). Therefore:

$$
1 - \lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2 = 1 - \sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}_i)(1-\mathcal{E}_j)} \le \mathcal{E}_i + \mathcal{E}_j
$$

By the Cauchy–Schwarz and AM–GM inequalities, the tail product is bounded by:

$$
|\langle t_i, t_j \rangle| \le \sqrt{\mathcal{E}_i \mathcal{E}_j} \le \frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}_i + \mathcal{E}_j)
$$

Substituting these numerators and applying the lower bound $\lVert u_i \rVert_2 \lVert u_j \rVert_2 \ge 1-c$ (since $\mathcal{E}_i, \mathcal{E}_j \le c$):

$$
|\tilde{s}_{ij}^{(d_k)} - s_{ij}| \le \frac{1.5 (\mathcal{E}_i + \mathcal{E}_j)}{1-c}
$$

Taking the expectation $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ over independent pairs $z_i, z_j$, we obtain $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_i] = \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_j] = \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R)$. Thus:

$$
\mathbb{E}\left[ \Delta_{ij}^{(d_k)} \right] \le \frac{1.5 (\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) + \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R))}{1-c} = \frac{3}{1-c} \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) \quad \blacksquare
$$

*Remark:* This exact $\mathcal{O}(\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k)$ bound provides query planners in Vector DBs with a tight, reliable guarantee for distortion decay, explicitly resolving the loose approximations typical in perturbative approaches.

#### Theorem 2 (The Extrinsic Curvature Gap)

Let $\mathcal{M} = \mathrm{supp}(\mu) \subset S^{D-1}$ be a connected, smooth Riemannian submanifold. Assume the intrinsic dimension satisfies $\dim(\mathcal{M}) \le d_k < D$.

If the **second fundamental form** $\mathrm{II}$ of $\mathcal{M}$ vanishes identically (i.e., $\mathcal{M}$ is contained in an affine subspace of dimension $d_k$), then there exists an orthogonal operator $R \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ such that $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) = 0$, allowing a zero-distortion linear embedding.

Conversely, if $\mathrm{II} \not\equiv 0$ (non‑zero extrinsic curvature), the minimal affine span required to contain $\mathcal{M}$ strictly exceeds its intrinsic dimension. If $\dim(\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{M})) > d_k$, no linear orthogonal operator $R \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ can achieve zero tail energy:

$$
\inf_{R \in \mathrm{O}(D)} \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) \ge \epsilon_{\mathcal{M}} > 0
$$

**Proof Sketch:**  
Suppose there exists $R^* \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ such that $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R^*) = 0$. This implies the entire support $\mathcal{M}$ is perfectly contained within a linear subspace $V_{d_k}$ of dimension $d_k$.

By the Gauss formula, the ambient directional derivative decomposes as  
$$
\nabla_X^{\mathbb{R}^D} Y = \nabla_X^{\mathcal{M}} Y + \mathrm{II}(X, Y).
$$
If $\mathcal{M} \subset V_{d_k}$, all directional derivatives must lie within $V_{d_k}$, forcing the normal component $\mathrm{II}(X, Y)$ to vanish identically in all directions orthogonal to $V_{d_k}$.

However, by hypothesis, the extrinsic curvature $\mathrm{II} \not\equiv 0$. This curvature forces the affine hull $\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{M})$ to expand strictly beyond $\dim(\mathcal{M})$. If the curvature causes $\dim(\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{M})) > d_k$, the manifold geometrically spills out of any $d_k$-dimensional linear subspace, dictating an irreducible residual energy $\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}} > 0$. $\blacksquare$

*Implication:* Extrinsic curvature is the sole geometric obstruction preventing a manifold from being linearly compressed without distortion. Eliminating this gap requires non-linear coordinate deformations learned end-to-end via native MRL.

### 4. Algorithmic Formulations: Closed-Form vs. Riemannian Optimization

```text
                                [ Calibration Set Z ∈ ℝ^(N×D) ]
                                               │
                      ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
                      ▼                                                 ▼
        [ Linear Trace Objective ]                          [ Spherical Cosine Objective ]
        min tr(Γ R C R^T)                                   min L_spherical(R)
                      │                                                 │
                      ▼ (Ky Fan Theorem)                                ▼ (Non-Linear Denominator)
        [ Closed-Form Solution (PCA) ]                      [ Riemannian Flag Optimization ]
        R* = Eigenvectors(C)                                Cayley Retraction on Fl(d_k)
        Complexity: O(N D^2 + D^3)                          Complexity: O(T · B · D^2)
```

#### 4.1 The Linear Limit: Ky Fan Equivalence

Consider minimizing the weighted expected Euclidean tail energy:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{linear}}(R) = \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} w_k \cdot \bar{\mathcal{E}}_k(R) = \operatorname{tr}(\Gamma R C R^\top)
$$

where $C = \frac{1}{N} Z^\top Z$ is the sample covariance matrix, and $\Gamma$ is a diagonal matrix containing cumulative weights (zero for the first $d_1$ entries, positive for tail dimensions).

**Proposition 4.1 (Closed-Form Optimality of PCA)**

By the Ky Fan theorem, the global minimum of $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma R C R^\top)$ over the orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(D)$ is achieved analytically when the rows of $R$ are the eigenvectors of $C$, sorted by **descending eigenvalues** (so that the largest eigenvalues align with the zero‑weight leading dimensions).  
*Significance:* If the goal is purely minimizing Euclidean residual energy, iterative optimization is computationally redundant. PCA solves the problem in a single $\mathcal{O}(D^3)$ step.

#### 4.2 The Non-Linear Spherical Cosine Objective

Retrieval fidelity depends on normalized spherical cosine distortion, not unnormalized Euclidean error. We define the true spherical loss over sampled pairs $\mathcal{P}$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{spherical}}(R) = \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} w_k \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{P}} \left( \frac{\langle \Pi_{d_k}(R z_i), \Pi_{d_k}(R z_j) \rangle}{\lVert \Pi_{d_k}(R z_i) \rVert_2 \lVert \Pi_{d_k}(R z_j) \rVert_2} - \langle z_i, z_j \rangle \right)^2
$$

Because the per-vector normalization factors $\lVert \Pi_{d_k}(R z) \rVert_2$ are non-linear in $R$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{spherical}}$ cannot be expressed as a linear trace. PCA provides only a first-order initialization; true optimality requires Riemannian gradient descent.

#### 4.3 Riemannian Flag Gradient Descent via Cayley Retraction

To minimize $\mathcal{L}_{\text{spherical}}(R)$ over $\mathrm{Fl}(d_1, \dots, d_K; \mathbb{R}^D)$, we compute the Euclidean gradient $G = \nabla_R \mathcal{L}_{\text{spherical}}$ via automatic differentiation and project it onto the tangent space $T_R \mathrm{O}(D)$:

$$
A_t = G_t R_t^\top - R_t G_t^\top \in \mathfrak{so}(D)
$$

We advance along the manifold geodesics using the Cayley transform:

$$
R_{t+1} = \left( I_D + \frac{\eta}{2} A_t \right)^{-1} \left( I_D - \frac{\eta}{2} A_t \right) R_t
$$

This ensures strict orthogonality ($R_{t+1}^\top R_{t+1} = I_D$) without post-hoc Gram-Schmidt re-orthogonalization passes.

```text
Algorithm 1: Riemannian Flag Gradient Descent
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input  : Embeddings Z ∈ ℝ^(N×D), target capacities {d_k}, weights {w_k}, lr η.
1: Initialize R_0 ← U^T, where U are eigenvectors of Z^T Z sorted descending.
2: for t = 0 to T - 1 do
3:     Compute Euclidean gradient G_t = ∇_R L_spherical(R_t) over mini-batch
4:     Construct skew-symmetric tangent vector A_t = G_t R_t^T - R_t G_t^T
5:     Compute Cayley update: R_{t+1} = (I_D + (η/2) A_t)^(-1) (I_D - (η/2) A_t) R_t
6: return R* = R_t
```

### 5. Experimental Protocol & Research Questions

The empirical framework aims to evaluate the gap between post-hoc rotation and native training:

* **RQ1 (Bound Validation):** Verify that empirical distortion obeys the hard analytical limit set by Theorem 1.
* **RQ2 (PCA vs. Riemannian Optimization):** Measure the retrieval improvement ($\Delta \mathrm{NDCG@10}$) of Algorithm 1 over the closed-form SVD baseline across intermediate capacities ($d_k \in \{64, 128\}$).
* **RQ3 (The Extrinsic Curvature Gap):** Quantify the remaining delta between Algorithm 1 and native, end-to-end retrained MRL models to validate Theorem 2 empirically.

Testbeds include standard retrieval benchmarks utilizing modern vision (DINOv3, CLIP) and text embeddings (ModernBERT, GTE-Qwen2).

### 6. Significance & Systems Impact

Post-hoc Matryoshkization bridges the gap between frozen deep learning representations and high-throughput vector databases:

1. **Zero-Retraining Cost:** Allows vector databases (like FAISS, Milvus, or Pithos) to reorder coordinate frames of billions of frozen vectors in a single offline calibration pass.
2. **Exact Full-Rank Invariance:** Because $R^* \in \mathrm{O}(D)$ is strictly orthogonal, retrieval quality at full rank $d_K = D$ is mathematically invariant ($\Delta = 0.00\%$).
3. **Analytical Pruning Guarantees:** The Spherical Flag Distortion Bound provides database query planners with provable worst-case guarantees on similarity errors, enabling latency-accuracy trade-offs during hierarchical pruning.
